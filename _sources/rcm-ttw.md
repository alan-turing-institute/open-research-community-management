# Contributing to *The Turing Way*

> **Note:** This page records how the Turing's Research Community Management team engaged with *The Turing Way*. *The Turing Way* remains an open, community-driven project that anyone, including RCMs anywhere in the world, can use and contribute to, so people seeking active community-management resources are encouraged to join [*The Turing Way*](https://the-turing-way.netlify.app/welcome) directly.

All members of the Open Research Community Management team were expected to learn the details in the [*The Turing Way* core documents](https://github.com/alan-turing-institute/open-research-community-management/tree/main/the-turing-way-core). Community managers were onboarded as core members of *The Turing Way*, and part of their time was allocated to the Tools, Practices and Systems (TPS) programme and *The Turing Way*. Malvika Sharan, who led the team from 2022 to 2024, led *The Turing Way* alongside Kirstie Whitaker, and Emma Karoune led the team from 2025 to 2026.

## Why RCMs engaged with *The Turing Way*

Engagement with *The Turing Way* project and community helps RCMs to:

- enable reproducibility, open communication, and ethical research in their projects and the Communities of Practice they build;
- identify people, projects, and resources that are crucial for the development, maintenance, and sustainability of their projects;
- interconnect their work with other projects and communities in the wider landscape of data science and open research;
- facilitate openness, collaboration, and transparent reporting, even when not all components of their projects can be made public;
- find support for their community work and, in exchange, support others' work, especially when it is closely aligned with their projects; and
- enhance the technical understanding required in their work and share it more widely via *The Turing Way* (chapters, tutorials, templates, or events).

## *The Turing Way* at the Turing

The Turing's programme page describes *The Turing Way* as the main reference for the RCM team. Anne Lee Steele was the Research Community Manager who facilitated collaborations across *The Turing Way* communities. Malvika Sharan also led *The Turing Way* Practitioners Hub, which was part of the Innovate UK BridgeAI programme, and Arielle Bennett, TPS Senior Researcher for Open Source Practices, worked on the hub.
